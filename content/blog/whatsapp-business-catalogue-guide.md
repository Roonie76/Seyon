The WhatsApp Business app is free, takes twenty minutes to set up, and solves several problems that cost small sellers real money. It is worth doing properly before you consider anything more complicated.

It also has a ceiling, and it is worth knowing where that ceiling is before you build your entire business under it.

## What to set up, in twenty minutes

**Business profile.** Name, category, a one-line description, your city, your hours, and an address if you have a physical one. Buyers check this. An empty profile reads as a personal number, and a personal number asking for a bank transfer is a risk.

**Catalogue.** Add every current item with a photograph, a name, a price, and a description. The single highest-value field is the price. A catalogue without prices is a photo album. ([Product photos taken on a phone](/blog/product-photography-with-a-phone) and [descriptions that answer real questions](/blog/how-to-write-a-product-description) are covered separately.)

**Greeting message.** One or two lines, sent automatically to a first-time messager. Use it to answer the question they were about to ask: *"Thanks for the message — prices are in the catalogue below, delivery is 4–6 days across India, and I reply between 10am and 7pm."*

**Away message.** Set hours and say when you will reply. "Back tomorrow morning" prevents the second message at 11pm asking if you saw the first.

**Quick replies.** Save the four answers you type twenty times a week — shipping time, payment method, care instructions, custom order policy — behind shortcuts. This is the single biggest hour-saver in the app.

**Labels.** New enquiry, awaiting payment, paid, shipped. Four labels. You will not use more, and with fewer you cannot see the state of the week.

> The catalogue does not sell for you. It stops you from re-typing the same four answers forty times a day, which is different and almost as valuable.

## What the catalogue is actually good at

It is good at the middle of a conversation. Someone asks what else you have; you send a catalogue link and they browse without leaving the chat. That is a real, meaningful improvement over sending eleven photographs.

It is also good for the buyer's confidence. A business profile with a stocked catalogue, hours, and a location is visibly a business.

## Where the ceiling is

The catalogue is a private room. Nobody arrives at it.

- **It is not indexed.** No search engine reads it, no AI assistant can cite it, no buyer finds it by searching for what you make. It is only ever seen by someone who already has your number.
- **You cannot link to one item cleanly from outside.** Sharing works best inside chat, which is exactly where the person already is.
- **There is no reviews layer.** A first-time buyer has no way to see that other people have bought from you and received their order.
- **You cannot search it the way a shopper would.** No filtering by price, no browsing by category across sellers.
- **It goes where the number goes.** Account restrictions, a lost SIM, a switched phone with a bad backup — the catalogue is tied to all of it.

None of these are flaws in WhatsApp. They are the correct design for a messaging app. It is a superb tool for *conversation with someone who already found you*, and it was never built to be the thing that finds them.

## The split that works

The clean way to think about it is: one place people arrive, one place people talk.

**Arriving** needs to be public, indexable, linkable, price-visible, and evidence-carrying. A stranger who has never heard of you should be able to land on it from a search, understand what you sell, see that it costs ₹1,450, see that eleven people have bought from you, and decide. That is the same argument as [the link in your bio is a dead end](/blog/the-link-in-your-bio-is-a-dead-end), approached from the other side.

**Talking** should stay in WhatsApp. Chat is not a workaround for a checkout; for most independent sellers it is the best part of the business. It is where you say the piece will be ready Thursday, where you suggest the smaller size, where you become someone a person buys from twice.

The mistake is not using WhatsApp. It is using WhatsApp for both jobs, so that every stranger's first four questions have to be answered by hand, and the ones who do not message you are invisible.

### A test worth running

Ask someone who has never bought from you to find your catalogue without you sending it to them.

They cannot. That is not a criticism of your setup — it is the design. Everything they *could* have found, on their own, before deciding to message you, is the part of your shop that does not exist yet.

## If you only change one thing

Add prices to every catalogue item, and put the same list somewhere public with a link you can paste anywhere.

The catalogue handles the conversation. The public page handles the arrival. Neither replaces the other, and most sellers who feel stuck have built only one of them.
