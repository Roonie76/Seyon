There is a particular class of object that survives being loved by a four-year-old and then sits on a shelf for forty years afterwards. Turned, lacquered wood is most of that class.

## Channapatna

A small town between Bengaluru and Mysuru that has been turning wooden toys for a couple of centuries. The craft grew under the patronage of Tipu Sultan, who admired woodwork; later, an artisan named Bavas Miyan introduced Japanese doll-making techniques that changed how the town produced.

The traditional wood is *Wrightia tinctoria*, known locally as **ivory-wood** or *hale mara* — pale, close-grained, and soft enough to turn cleanly. Rubber wood, cedar, pine and teak are also used now.

What makes the finish distinctive is that it is not paint. The piece is turned on a lathe, and while it is still spinning a stick of coloured lac is pressed against it. Friction melts the lac onto the wood. It is then burnished — traditionally with a screw-pine leaf — until it shines.

So the colour is fused into a hard lacquer skin rather than sitting on top as a film. It does not flake the way paint does; it wears thin, which is a much nicer way to age.

**The colours are vegetable-dyed and the toys are made to be safe for a child to put in their mouth.** That is the reason the craft persisted while painted toys fell out of favour, and it is why adults now buy them for rooms with no children in them.

The craft holds a geographical indication, meaning the name is protected to pieces genuinely made there.

> Nothing else in the toy aisle is a lathe-turned hardwood object finished in fused resin. It just happens to cost less than the plastic.

## Etikoppaka

The same idea, four hundred kilometres away in Andhra Pradesh, and a different lineage.

Etikoppaka work is turned from *ankudu* wood and lacquered with dyes derived from seeds, roots, bark and leaves. The village is closely associated with a revival of natural dyeing after chemical colours had largely displaced it.

The forms are different from Channapatna's: more figurative, more storytelling — animals, deities, ox carts, spinning tops, nested pieces. The palette runs warmer and earthier because of the natural dyes.

## What to look for

**Turn it over and look at the base.** A lathe-turned piece has a small mark at the centre where it was held. Absolutely uniform pieces with a seam running around them are moulded plastic, and there are imitations.

**Look at the colour boundary.** On real lac work, where two colours meet there is a slight softness — one melted against the other on a spinning form. Printed or painted decoration has a hard edge.

**Feel the surface.** Fused lac is smooth and slightly warm, with a depth to the shine. Varnish over paint feels like a coating, and you can often see a meniscus where it pooled.

**Weight and sound.** Hardwood has heft and a solid knock. Hollow plastic does not.

## Living with it

These are genuinely robust, with three real vulnerabilities.

**Water.** Lac softens and clouds with prolonged wet. Wipe with a dry or barely damp cloth; never soak, never dishwasher.

**Heat.** Direct sun over months will fade vegetable dyes, and a hot surface can soften the lacquer. Keep it off a sunny windowsill.

**Impact.** It will dent on stone. Wood dents rather than shatters, which is why it works for children.

Otherwise: nothing. No polishing, no oiling, no maintenance. A Channapatna piece bought for a child routinely outlives the childhood and gets handed to the next one.

## What to buy first

For a child, the classic small forms — stacking rings, a rattle, a spinning top, a pull-along animal. They are inexpensive enough to be unprecious and well made enough to survive.

For a room, the plain turned vessels and the larger figures. A row of lacquered bowls in graduating sizes does a great deal of work on a shelf for very little money.

For a [gift to someone you do not know well](/blog/gifting-guide-india), this whole category is close to unbeatable — visually charming, culturally specific, harmless to anyone's taste, and cheap to post.

The one thing to check before you order is size. These photograph large and frequently arrive smaller than people expect, which is a disappointment entirely avoidable by reading the dimensions.
