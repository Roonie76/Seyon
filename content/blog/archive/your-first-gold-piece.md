Buying gold for the first time with your own money is a strange transaction. It is the most expensive thing most people have bought that has no function, and the shop is set up on the assumption that you already know what you are doing.

You do not have to. Six decisions cover it.

## One: decide what it is for before you decide what it is

There are three honest reasons to buy gold, and they lead to different purchases.

**To wear.** Then everything below applies, and the piece should be something you will put on in an ordinary week.

**To keep.** A store of value, to be sold or remade later. Then you want maximum metal for minimum making charge — a plain chain or plain bangles, not an elaborate piece.

**To mark something.** A first salary, a graduation, a birth. Then the piece can be impractical, because its job is to exist rather than to be worn daily.

Most first purchases fail because the buyer wanted the third and bought as though they wanted the first.

## Two: karat is a durability decision, not just a purity one

**22K** is 91.6% gold. Traditional in India, richly yellow, and soft. Wonderful for pieces worn occasionally. A 22K ring worn every day will go slightly out of round and pick up dents.

**18K** is 75% gold, noticeably harder, slightly paler. The sensible choice for a ring or anything with stones, because it holds a setting.

**14K** is 58.5%, harder again, and the most durable of the three. Less common in Indian retail, more common in contemporary design.

Higher karat is not better. It is softer and more expensive. For a first piece you intend to wear, 18K is usually the right answer, and almost nobody will suggest it to you.

## Three: understand what you are paying for

A gold price has four parts and any decent seller will break them out:

1. **Metal.** Weight in grams multiplied by the day's rate for that karat.
2. **Making charges.** The labour. Charged per gram or as a percentage, and it varies enormously — a plain chain might be 5%, an intricate piece 25% or more.
3. **Stones**, if any, priced separately.
4. **GST.**

Making charges are the number to ask about, because they are the part that is not recoverable. If you sell or exchange the piece later, you get metal value; the making charge is gone. This is why a plain heavy chain is the efficient way to hold gold and an elaborate piece is not.

> Ask for the weight, the rate, and the making charge as three separate numbers. A seller who will not separate them is not somewhere to buy your first gold.

## Four: check the hallmark yourself

Hallmarking gold has been mandatory in India since 2021. A hallmarked piece carries three marks:

- The **BIS triangle**.
- The **purity and fineness** — "22K916", "18K750".
- A **six-character HUID**, unique to that item.

Type the HUID into the BIS Care app and it returns the purity, the jeweller and the hallmarking centre. Do it in the shop, before paying. It takes thirty seconds and it is the only part of the transaction with an enforcement body behind it.

What the hallmark does *not* cover: the stones, the design, or whether the making charge is fair. It certifies metal, nothing more.

## Five: get the buyback terms in writing

Ask what the shop pays if you bring the piece back, and get the answer on the invoice. Policies vary widely — some deduct a percentage, some pay current metal rate, some only offer exchange against a new purchase.

This matters more than it sounds. Gold is one of the few things you buy expecting to possibly unbuy, and the terms are set at the moment of sale, not later.

## Six: buy the boring thing

For a first piece, the highest satisfaction per rupee is almost always something plain that you can wear with anything: small hoops, a pair of studs, a plain chain, a single flat band.

The temptation is the opposite — to make a first gold purchase *significant*, which usually means large, ornate and occasion-bound. Those pieces get worn twice a year. A plain gold hoop gets worn eight hundred times.

If you want the significant piece, buy it second, once you have learned what you actually reach for. [The same order works for metal generally.](/blog/gold-vs-silver-which-suits-you)

## A word on inherited gold

If there is family gold sitting unworn, that is a cheaper and more interesting first piece than anything in a shop — a goldsmith can remake it, and you pay only making charges on metal you already own. [It is worth looking properly before you buy new.](/blog/restyling-inherited-jewellery)
