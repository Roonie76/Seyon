There is jewellery you own and jewellery you wear. For most people the gap between the two is embarrassing — a drawer of pieces bought for occasions that came once, and the same three things going on every morning without thinking.

The three things are worth studying, because they tell you what to buy next.

## What everyday jewellery has to survive

Not much, until you list it. Sleep. Showers. Hand wash forty times a day. Sunscreen. A dupatta pulled over your head. A laptop bag strap. Children. Getting caught on a sleeve and yanked.

Anything that cannot take that becomes occasion jewellery whether you meant it to or not. So the everyday question is not "is this beautiful" — it is **what happens to this piece on an ordinary Tuesday.**

Three properties decide it:

**Low profile.** Anything that projects catches. A flat or slightly domed ring survives; a raised claw setting snags on knitwear and loses stones. Studs and small hoops survive; long drops get pulled.

**A closure you trust.** The single most common way people lose jewellery is a clasp that opens under a bag strap. A screw-back stud, a lever-back earring, an S-hook or a good spring ring — these are worth paying for. A cheap open jump ring is not a clasp.

**A material that ages rather than degrades.** Solid metal wears smooth and looks better. Plating wears through and looks worse. This is the whole argument for solid silver or gold over plated pieces for anything you wear daily.

> Occasion jewellery is judged on how it looks. Everyday jewellery is judged on how it looks after two years.

## The pieces that actually stay on

Across most people who wear jewellery daily, the rotation is small and predictable:

1. **Small studs or huggie hoops.** Put in and forgotten. If you buy one thing for daily wear, this is it.
2. **A thin chain, worn short.** Long chains swing into things and tangle in bag straps. 16–18 inches sits under a collar and stays put.
3. **One or two stacking rings.** Plain bands take an enormous amount of abuse. Stones do not.
4. **A single bangle or a flat cuff.** Something with no moving parts.
5. **A thin, flat-linked bracelet**, if you type all day — anything thicker sits on the desk edge and scratches.

Notice what is not on that list: statement earrings, cocktail rings, layered necklaces, anything with a pendant heavier than the chain. Those are wonderful and they are for occasions.

### The one-week test

Wear a piece for seven consecutive days before deciding whether you love it. Almost everything passes on day one. The failures show up around day four — it turns, it catches, it presses into your neck when you lie down, the clasp migrates to the front.

## Where to spend and where not to

**Spend on the earrings.** They are on the longest, they are the most visible, and a good pair of studs is the highest-mileage jewellery purchase available. Solid metal, and a back you can tighten.

**Spend on the chain, not the pendant.** A weak chain fails and you lose both. A good chain outlives several pendants.

**Do not spend on the trend piece.** The chunky-whatever of this year will be in the drawer by next. Buy it inexpensive or buy it in silver.

**Spend on one good ring rather than five cheap ones.** Rings get looked at closely, from a distance of two feet, by people you are talking to.

## Metal, honestly

For daily wear the choice comes down to how much you mind maintenance.

**Gold** does not tarnish. It is the low-effort choice and it is expensive. Lower karats (18K, 14K) are harder and better for daily wear than 22K, which is soft enough to distort on a ring worn constantly.

**Sterling silver** tarnishes and then stops looking new — though skin contact keeps worn silver bright, so daily silver stays better than silver in a box. It is a fraction of the price, and [there is more to know about buying it than most sellers volunteer](/blog/how-to-buy-silver-jewellery-india).

**Plated anything** is a poor daily choice at any price. The plating wears through at the exact points that touch you most, which means the piece looks worst where it is most visible.

**Brass, copper and German silver** will mark some skin green. That is a harmless reaction between metal, sweat and whatever you put on your hands, not a sign of a bad piece — but it is a reason those metals belong in occasion jewellery rather than daily.

## Buying it from a maker

An independent silversmith or goldsmith is the best place to get daily-wear jewellery, because you can ask for the two things that decide whether it survives: **a heavier gauge** and **a better closure.** Both are usually available and rarely offered by default.

Ask directly: *can you make this in a thicker wire, and can I have screw backs instead of push backs?* The answer is often yes, for very little more money, and it is the difference between a piece you own for two years and one you own for twenty.
